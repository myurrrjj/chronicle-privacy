Privacy Policy for Chronicle

Last updated: 2 November 2026 · Applies to Chronicle for Android, version 1.0 and later

Chronicle is an Android photo and video gallery. This policy explains exactly what the app accesses, where that information goes, and the control you have over it. It is written to be read, not to be skipped — and every claim in it can be checked against the app itself.

In short

  • Chronicle has no server, no account and no sign-in. There is nothing to register for and nothing of yours stored by us.
  • The app does not request the Android INTERNET permission. It therefore cannot send your photos, videos, face data or anything else off your device. This is enforced by Android, not just promised by us.
  • Everything Chronicle works out stays on your phone: your favourites, your search index, your settings, and the face grouping data used to sort photos by the people in them.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking SDKs. No third-party company receives data from this app.
  • You can delete everything at any time by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it. There is no copy anywhere else for us to delete.

1. Who we are and what this policy covers

Chronicle is developed and published by Mayur Jindal, an independent developer ("we", "us"). This policy covers the Chronicle Android application (package com.myurrrjj.chronicle) and nothing else. It does not cover other apps, websites or services you may reach from Chronicle, including any app you choose to share a photo with.

Because Chronicle has no account and no server, we do not hold a database of users. The personal information described in this policy is processed on your own device, under your control, and is never transmitted to us.

2. What Chronicle accesses on your device

Chronicle reads your media library through Android's MediaStore — the same system index your phone's built-in gallery uses. It reads only the photos and videos you have allowed it to see, and only to display them.

WhatWhyWhere it goes

Location is not read. Chronicle does not read GPS data from your photos, does not request any location permission, and does not show or store where a photo was taken.

3. Face grouping, and what it does and does not do

Chronicle can group the photos in your library by the people in them, so that every photo of the same person sits together. This is the most sensitive thing the app does, so here is the full account of it.

How it works

  • While you use the gallery, a background task looks through your photos for faces. While this is running, Android shows a notification ("Gallery: Finding People") because the work continues briefly even if you switch away. You can turn notifications off for Chronicle in Android settings.
  • Faces are found using Google ML Kit's face detection, in its bundled form: the detection model is shipped inside the app and runs entirely on your phone. Nothing is sent to Google or downloaded from Google at runtime.
  • Each detected face is turned into a face embedding — a list of numbers that describes the geometry of that face — using a TensorFlow Lite model (sface_embedder_fp32.tflite) that is also bundled inside the app.
  • Embeddings that are mathematically similar are grouped together by an on-device clustering algorithm. That grouping is what appears in the app as "people".
  • The group names are just labels. If the app shows a name for a group, it is a label you typed, or a placeholder. Chronicle does not know, guess, or look up who anyone actually is.

What is stored, and where

The face embeddings, the bounding boxes of the faces in each photo, the groups they were sorted into and the processing status of each item are stored in a private database inside the app's own storage. They are never uploaded, never shared, and never sent to us or anyone else.

What Chronicle does not do with faces

  • It does not identify anyone. There is no matching against any database of real people, and no name is ever attached to a face by the app.
  • It does not use faces for authentication, unlocking, or any security decision.
  • It does not send face data, face crops, embeddings or derived templates anywhere. The app has no internet permission, so it could not even if it tried.
  • It does not use your photos or face data to train any model. The models are fixed and shipped inside the app.

How to remove face data

Clearing Chronicle's storage (Android Settings → Apps → Chronicle → Storage & cache → Clear storage) deletes the entire face database along with everything else the app has saved. Uninstalling the app does the same. Because face data is derived from photos that remain in your library, it is not recreated unless the app indexes your library again after you reinstall.

Version 1.0 groups faces automatically as part of normal use and does not include a separate on/off switch. If you would prefer not to have this happen, the effective options today are to clear the app's storage after using it, or to uninstall it. We intend to add a control for this in a future version; this policy will be updated when we do.

5. What is stored on your device

Everything Chronicle saves lives in the app's private storage area, which other apps cannot read:

  • A local database holding your favourites, the face embeddings and people groups described above, which items have been processed, and a search index of terms so search is fast.
  • Your settings — whether videos autoplay, and which view mode you prefer.
  • An image cache in the app's cache directory, so thumbnails and photos load quickly when you scroll. Android and the app clear this cache as space is needed; you can also clear it yourself from Android's storage settings.

Chronicle does not copy your photos or videos into its own storage. It reads them where they already are. The one exception is the thumbnail cache described above, which is a display cache, not a copy of your library.

6. What Chronicle does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in, profiles or cloud sync.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect data. The only Google components are ML Kit's face detection and TensorFlow Lite, both of which run entirely on your device.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your information — we never receive it.
  • No access to your contacts, location, microphone, calendar or messages.
  • No upload of your photos, videos, metadata or face data to any server, ours or anyone else's.

7. Third parties and other apps

There is no third party that receives your data from Chronicle. For completeness, here is what is involved in making the app work:

  • Google ML Kit (face detection) and TensorFlow Lite — on-device machine learning libraries bundled inside the app. They process images locally and have no network access in this app.
  • Android system components — MediaStore for reading your library, WorkManager for the background indexing task, and the system notification service. These are part of your phone's operating system.
  • Google Play — when you install or update Chronicle, Google processes that transaction under Google's own privacy policy. We receive only the aggregate, anonymous statistics that Google Play provides to every developer (such as install counts and crash-free rates), never anything that identifies you.

If you use the share sheet to send a photo to another app — a chat app, a cloud drive, an editor — that app receives the file, and its own privacy policy governs what happens next. That transfer is always started by you, one file at a time, with a system confirmation.

8. Android backup

Android can back up app data to your own Google account, so that your apps come back when you set up a new phone. Chronicle allows this, which means your favourites, your settings and the face grouping database may be included in your Android backup.

That backup belongs to your Google account and is controlled by your device's backup settings — not by us, and we cannot read it. You can turn it off, or delete existing backups, in Android Settings → System → Backup, or in your Google account settings. If you would rather nothing at all left the device in any form, turn Android backup off for this app, or clear the app's storage before you stop using it.

9. Sharing, editing and the clipboard

  • Share and edit — when you share a photo or video, or send it to an editor, Chronicle hands that single file to the app you choose through Android's share sheet, with temporary read permission. It does not share anything else, and it does not keep a record of what you shared.
  • Copy — the copy action puts a reference to the selected item on the system clipboard, so you can paste it into another app. The clipboard is managed by Android; other apps you paste into will receive it.
  • Delete — deleting a photo or video is done through Android's own deletion dialog. Chronicle asks the system to delete the item; Android shows you the confirmation. Chronicle cannot delete anything without that confirmation on modern Android versions.

10. Deletion and retention

We do not retain your data, because we never receive it. There is no server-side copy and no backup of ours. On your device:

  • Delete individual media in the app, with the system confirmation prompt. This removes the file from your device's library.
  • Clear the app's data — Android Settings → Apps → Chronicle → Storage & cache → Clear storage. This erases the database (favourites, face data, search index) and your settings. Your photos and videos are untouched.
  • Uninstall Chronicle — removes everything the app ever stored on your device.

If you have Android backup enabled, a copy of the app's data may persist in your Google account backup until that backup is replaced or deleted — see section 8 for how to control it.

Chronicle is not a backup service. It is a viewer for the media already on your phone. Deleting a file in Chronicle deletes it from your device. Keep your own backups of anything you would be sorry to lose.

11. Security

Because nothing is transmitted, there is no data in transit to protect. On the device, Chronicle's database, settings and cache sit in the app's private storage, which Android isolates from other apps. Chronicle does not request the internet permission, so no code path in the app can send your data out.

No system is perfectly secure. Someone with physical access to an unlocked device, a rooted device, or malware with elevated privileges could potentially reach app data. Chronicle has no separate passcode or lock of its own, so the security of your photos is the security of your phone — keep your device lock on and your OS updated.

12. Children

Chronicle is a general-purpose gallery, not directed at children, and it has no accounts, no social features, no advertising and no way to communicate with anyone. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children, because the app does not collect information off the device at all.

Your photo library may of course contain images of children. Those images are processed exactly like any other image — on your device, never uploaded — and face grouping on them stays on the device too.

13. Your rights and choices

Privacy laws such as India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the GDPR in Europe, give you rights over personal data — to know what is processed, to access it, to correct it, and to have it erased. In Chronicle these rights are exercised directly on your device, because that is where all the data is:

  • Access and portability — your photos and videos remain in your own library, readable by any gallery app. Chronicle does not hold them.
  • Correction — edit or rename anything through your device's file tools or another gallery app; Chronicle re-reads the library.
  • Erasure — clear the app's storage or uninstall it (see section 10).
  • Withdrawing access — revoke the photo and video permissions, or switch Chronicle to limited access, in Android Settings → Apps → Chronicle → Permissions. The app will simply show fewer items.
  • Questions or complaints — write to the address in section 15 and we will answer.
